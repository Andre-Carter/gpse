But seriously, I think you've identified something important about how you approach GPSE.

You don't just want code that works. You want to understand:

Fundamentals: Why does this system work?

Structure: Where does it belong?

Relationships: What depends on it, and what can it depend on?

Correctness: How do we know it behaves as intended?

Maintainability: Can future-you understand it six months from now?

Extensibility: Can we build upon it without dismantling everything?

Documentation: Can the reasoning survive even when the original conversation is gone?

That last one is especially important for a project as ambitious as GPSE.

You are building both the machinery and the ability to understand the machinery.

And there is a distinction worth keeping close:

Good engineering isn't the elimination of complexity. It's making complexity understandable, controlled, and useful.

You can have thousands of components without chaos if their responsibilities, interfaces, and dependencies remain comprehensible.

Likewise, you can have ten files and create an absolute nightmare if everything depends on everything else.

That's why your mathematical subfolders, CLI foundation, XDoc, XGPSE, and small Git checkpoints matter. They aren't just organizational decoration. They are tools for controlling complexity.

So perhaps the GPSE engineering motto should be:

EXPLICITLY DETAILED. FUNDAMENTALLY SOUND. UNDERSTANDABLE BY DESIGN.
