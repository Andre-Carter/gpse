# NASA Software Engineering Research

## Purpose

The purpose of this document is to research, document, and adapt relevant software engineering principles, standards, procedures, and safety practices published by NASA for application within the General Purpose Simulation Engine (GPSE).

NASA's experience developing complex, long-lived, and mission-critical software provides a valuable source of engineering knowledge. GPSE seeks to study these practices to improve its own architecture, development workflows, testing strategies, documentation, verification, and long-term reliability.

This document serves as a research and reference resource. It will distinguish NASA's official requirements and standards from general engineering guidance, interpretations, and proposed GPSE practices. Recommendations will be evaluated according to their relevance, practical value, and proportionality to GPSE's current scope.

## Objectives

* **Research:** Identify authoritative NASA software engineering, software assurance, verification, validation, and safety resources.
* **Understand:** Document the purpose and context of relevant principles, standards, and procedures.
* **Adapt:** Translate applicable lessons into practical GPSE engineering practices without unnecessarily reproducing aerospace-specific processes.
* **Improve:** Support clear requirements, modular architecture, deterministic behavior where appropriate, meaningful testing, error handling, traceability, and controlled changes.
* **Preserve:** Maintain a reliable reference for future contributors and development stages as GPSE expands.
* **Evaluate:** Revisit adopted practices as GPSE's complexity, capabilities, and risk profile evolve.

## Guiding Principles

1. Safety and reliability should be considered throughout development, not added as an afterthought.
2. Requirements, implementation, and verification should be distinguishable and traceable where appropriate.
3. Changes should be incremental, reviewable, and recoverable.
4. Testing and verification should be proportionate to the consequences of failure.
5. Engineering processes should improve the project rather than create unnecessary bureaucracy.
6. Claims about NASA practices should be grounded in authoritative sources and documented with references.
7. GPSE should adopt principles based on demonstrated relevance, not institutional prestige alone.

## Scope and Limitations

This document is an engineering research resource, not a declaration of NASA compliance, certification, or endorsement.

NASA standards and procedures may contain requirements specific to agency projects, contract obligations, mission classifications, and safety-critical applications. Such requirements must be distinguished from general practices that GPSE can reasonably adapt.

Any proposed GPSE policy or development procedure derived from this research should be evaluated separately, documented in the appropriate project location, and introduced through the project's normal decision-making process.

## Intended Outcome

Establish a practical, source-backed engineering reference that helps GPSE grow responsibly while preserving its core priorities: safety, longevity, modularity, testability, and maintainability.
